## The miss, split

`arrLitShort` answered one boolean, and the IR reported its refusal as `array long or seeded`.
`arrLitShortWhy` now names the reason. Measured at the door, over 12,305 `distilled` cells, 7,298
non-`@error` `tests/cases` programs and the self-compile:

| reason | corpus + cases | self-compile |
|---|---:|---:|
| literal-union `atom elements` | 672 | 79 |

| `long` (past `NEW_FIXED_MAX`, 10,000, or the string chunk cap) | 14 | 0 |

So "long" is the rare case. `arrLitShort` had no caller left, and is deleted.

## Literal-union lists

Legacy's `emitArrI32` builds a `K[]` literal's elements with `emitExpr` and calls the same short
tail as any short list. An element literal lowers as its interned atom id. The exclusion was the
IR's own, from before its delivery took atoms. The IR delivers the atom itself
(`strLitTakesAtom`), so a literal-union list now takes the short build. At the door, the matched
count went from 41,600 to **42,283 (+683)**, with **0 differ**. The leftover atom misses are named
classes of their own: `atom string source` 35, `pre-path atom hook` 5, `expression if atom join` 2.
