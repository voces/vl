#### Mechanism

**It is not the annotate-everything split the filing assumed, and not [D1831](D1831.md)'s
family.** The annotated spelling (`const rs: (S | null)[]`, `const rd: S | null`) refused
identically, and D1831's own guard matrix reads 29 of 44 unchanged by this close. What
`indexOf` contributes is only that its signature is `(xs: T[], t: T)`: **one instance carrying
BOTH shapes** is the whole ingredient. `two<T>(xs: T[]): i32` alone runs, `two<T>(t: T): i32`
alone runs, and two SEPARATE generics — one per shape — run.

Printed at the type section's parameter valtype, the instance's second parameter reads

    PROBE paramsig fn=two kind=nulstruct t:(S|null)|emit={w:i32}|null|row=-1

— the annotation text is the PARENTHESISED `(S|null)`, and `retStructRowIndex` of it is -1.
The parens come from the binding: a `T[]` parameter binds `T` to `arrElemNameRaw` of the
argument's pin, deliberately raw so `bind + "[]"` re-spells the array. A bare `T` parameter
then pins that binding verbatim, and `(S|null)` is a spelling no interner has seen.

The close stores the binding UNGROUPED (`listElemNameOf`) and puts the grouping back at every
site that suffixes a `[]` — `arrElemSuffixed` in `tyname.vl`, which is [D1836](D1836.md)'s rule
promoted from the array-literal channel to the one home the three sites share, the third being
`monoSubstAnn`'s `b + "[]"`. That is why the fix also clears the OTHER parameter order, whose
ungrouped `S|null[]` refused with a different sentence.
