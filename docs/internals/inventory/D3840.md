### D3840 — a nested generic declaring the same type-parameter name as its enclosing generic cannot tell the two apart, since instances substitute type variables by name

**closed (2026-10-08, lane SC) · was check-clean invalid wasm, and a silent `1` for `true` with one instance · found in review of lane SC · filed 2026-10-08 against master `a6c18eb0e` · pins: `tests/cases/generics/nested-generic-shadowed-param-capture.vl`, `tests/cases/generics/nested-generic-capture-callers-that-run.vl`**

#### Mechanism

Every instance-binding table (`monoPinSubN`, `monoExactSubN`) and every reader of one
substitutes a `TyVar` by its NAME, so inside `inner`'s instance the captured `ys: T[]` read
`inner`'s `T`. The checker already keeps the two apart: each declaration of `T` mints its own
`TyVar` row, and a read inside `inner` carries the row of the scope that declared it. The fix,
`monoShadowRename` in `compiler/emit_mono.vl`, renames the enclosing declarer's rows (those its
annotations resolve to) to `T__sh<slot>` before monomorphization, and every instance of that
declarer binds the alias beside `T` (`monoShAliasBind`). A read typed at the outer `T` then
finds the outer pin walking up the enclosing instances, and the nested generic is keyed by its
enclosing instance like any other capturing one.
