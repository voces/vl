### D16490 — a literal `let` stored an `f32` becomes `f64` when a float literal also joins its stores, or when the literal is a float

**closed · runs, prints `1.399999976158142` · was check-clean silently wrong · clause 1 · the binding was typed `f64`, so its arithmetic ran in f64 · found by the independent oracle while reviewing #8271 · filed 2026-11-15 against master `7bf2afc7a` · closed 2026-11-15 by lane N: a stored float literal adopts a typed float store and makes the binding `f64` only where none is (`lbiFloatStoreDef`), and a float-literal `let` adopts the float stored into it rather than joining it with its own `f64` (`lbiChoose`) · fixture `inference/literal-let-joins-float-store.vl` · matrix `literal-let-joins-float-store.matrix.vl` 76 of 76 (68 before)**

Repro:

    const f: f32 = 1.5
    let t = 0
    t = f
    t = t - 0.1
    print(t)

Want:

    1.399999976158142

#### Mechanism

Ruling 4.3 at stores (D16293 (a), DECISIONS "A float stored into an integer literal `let` joins
it") makes a literal `let` the type of the typed floats stored into it, and a literal beside a
typed value adopts that value's type. #8250's `lbiFloatStoreDef` (check_num_lit_state.vl) gives
`f32` only "when every stored float is one and nothing else is stored", and `f64` "beside a float
literal or an `i32`". It classes the store `t - 0.1` as a float-literal store, although its
literal sits beside `t` and adopts `t`'s type, so one `f32` store and that store join to `f64`.
A float-literal `let` (`let t = 0.0`) does not reach `lbiFloatStoreDef`, which handles integer
candidates only, and its stored `f32` also leaves it `f64`. The rule owed (coordinator, 2026-11-15):
the `0.1` beside `t` adopts `t`'s type, and a float-literal `let` stored only `f32` is `f32`.

#### Ablation

`kind(t)` printed after the stores, with `const f: f32 = 1.5`; the oracle gives `f32` in every row.
