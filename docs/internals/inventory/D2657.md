#### Mechanism

The direct spelling `{[string]: boolean} | {[string]: i32}` is refused at check where an `is`
has to tell the two apart (D2493): both values are one i32 cell, so the two maps share one map
struct and one box tag. Inside the generic the checker sees `V | {[string]: i32}` and has
nothing to refuse; at the pin `V := {[string]: boolean}` the instance's union collapses the
two members onto the one mono map, and the `is` and the box disagree about the member set.

#### Close

The prediction that D2493's close closes this was half right. Two mechanisms, each needed:

* the instance's `let u: V | {[K]: i32}` annotation was substituted to a union spelling that no
  pass registered (`collectU` runs before the monomorphizer, which registered only a RETURN's
  minted union, D1042), so `u` lowered as a bare map — the invalid wasm at `V := string`, and at
  `V := {[string]: string}` on master too. The substituted local and parameter annotations now
  register it (`monoPinMintedUnion`), which also closes the parameter face `T | IM | string` at
  `T := BM` (`tests/cases/generics/pin-param-union-with-map-member.vl`);
* with the union registered, the pair shares one struct, and D2493's per-member tags are what
  let `is` answer.
