### D7500 — `vl check <dir>` re-checks the whole import graph of every root no other root imports

**runs today and must keep running · open · a cost row, no wrong value: the witness prints its Want, and the cost is the directory's multiplicity · owner lane S5 · filed against master `821360f93`**

Repro:

    import { includes } from "std:array"
    print(includes([1, 2, 3], 2))
    // The cost needs a directory whose roots import nothing in common with an importer: diderven's src/ has 110.

Want:

    true

#### Mechanism

Whole-root serving (`wiBankWhole`, driver.vl) serves a root only from an importer's passing
check. On diderven, 110 of the 330 files in `src/` are imported by no other file (102 are
tests), so nothing banks them, and each one checks its whole import graph again. Under
`VL_FUEL=1 VL_CHECK_STATS=1` at two workers this is 114 to 119 roots and about 5,300 of
9,250 module checks, against 3,630 served.

A batch check was tried and dropped: one synthetic entry importing several unseen roots, its
banks used to serve each root afterwards. A batch banks nothing when any root in it has a
diagnostic or meets another's demands, so on diderven it banked 1 to 4 roots and the total went
9,446 → 9,597 to 9,631 module checks.

#### Per-module serving, measured and dropped

Serving an unseen root's imports one module at a time was tried. In the root's check, a
top-level, non-generic function whose parameters and return are all annotated was not
body-checked, so the check read only its signature. This ran only when the function's module
already had a whole-root bank (`wiBanks`) and every source in that bank's closure was
unchanged. After the check, `wiGuard` ran again on each skipped module's closure, with the
root as the outside, and the module's banked lint sites took the place of the ones that were
skipped. Guest fuel under `VL_FUEL=1`, `vl check src` on a copy of diderven:

| jobs | master | skip, bank-gated | skipped modules | guard declines | roots needing a re-check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 115,800,937,459 | 116,129,146,541 (+0.3%) | 1,697 of 5,276 | 1,313, all `ambient` | 46 of 65 |
| 2 | 132,369,107,411 | 137,651,891,171 (+4.0%) | 554 of 5,318 | 95, all `ambient` | 18 of 91 |

This saves nothing, for two reasons. First, the bank covers few imports, and the ones it
covers are cheap ones. Second, `ambient` declines most of what it does cover, so a sound
version would also have to re-check those roots in full, and that cost is not in the table.

The skip without the bank gate gives the bound. Skipping every such body of every import:
110.6G at two jobs (−16.4%). Skipping every import body: 102.0G (−23%, and the exit code
changes). Neither is sound, and two programs show why:

- An import body can fix a declaration the root reads. `export let xs = []` with
  `export function add(s: string): i32 { xs.push(s); 0 }`, imported by `xs.push(1)`, reports
  `push: cannot add i32 to string[]` at master, but checks clean when `add`'s body is skipped.
- A bank built from a check that skipped bodies loses that module's own hints. Diderven's
  `reserving` (`char/mana.vl`), and `contactIn` and `stalled` (`sim/balance/policy.vl`),
  disappear.

Two body facts do survive the skip: a callee's writes that end a narrowing (the write scan is
syntactic), and a module-level guard function, which does not narrow across modules either way.

What decides how much per-module serving can save is what decides whole-root serving:
`ambient` (D7501). Until that guard reads less, neither kind of serving reaches the unseen
roots' imports.

#### Grading list

The fix is to serve a root's imports one module at a time from a shared bank: a module checked
once under the same importer demands is not checked again. Grade it with `vl check src` over a
copy of diderven under `VL_CHECK_STATS=1`, counting `unseen` module checks, at byte-identical
output against master at 1, 2 and 6 jobs.
