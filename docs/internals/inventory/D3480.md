### D3480 — about 2,000 occurrences of a wide float literal like `1e300` trap the compiler, each one costing 20 M fuel and 3.5 MB of never-freed heap to encode

**closed · runs, prints `2000` · was compiler trap (`allocation size too large` under the null collector, exit 70) · found by the #3329 review · filed 2026-10-03 against master `a8c4dce6d` · fixture `tests/cases/literals/many-wide-float-literals.vl` · closed 2026-10-03**

Repro:

    const xs = [@@repeat(1999, "1e300, ")@@1e300]
    print(xs.length)
    // vl check: rc 0.  vl build: "the compiler itself trapped", allocation size too large

Want:

    2000

#### Mechanism

`ieeeBytes` (compiler/emit_bignum.vl) encodes a decimal literal exactly, as `mant * 10^dexp`
divided by an aligned power of two, by bitwise long division (`bigDivmod`). Each of its ~1,000
steps for `1e300` built three fresh bignums (`bigShl1`, `bigAddSmall`, `bigSub`), the walk started
at the numerator's top bit although every step above `bitlen(num) - bitlen(den)` yields a zero
quotient bit, `bigShlK` aligned the denominator one bit at a time, and `10^300` was built one
digit at a time. Under the null collector nothing is freed, so ~2,000 such literals (as list
elements or as 5,000 `const`s) exhausted the 4 GiB GC heap. The division now reduces one remainder
in place and starts at the first bit that can set a quotient bit, the shift moves whole limbs,
and the power of ten grows four digits a step. Output is byte-identical: 2,018 random and
edge-case `f64` literals (subnormals, halfway cases, the max, 1e-400) and 1,506 `f32` ones build
the same module on both seeds.

#### Ablation

`vl build`, `VL_FUEL=1`, `VL_COMPILE_GC=null`.

| witness | master | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 500 × `1e300` | 9.8 G fuel, 1.8 GB | 0.82 G fuel (fixed cost ~0.2 G) |
| 1,000 × `1e300` | 19.6 G fuel, 3.6 GB | — |
| 2,000 × `1e300` | trap | 3.26 G fuel, 228 MB |
| 5,000 `const c = 1e300` | trap | 4.24 G fuel, 287 MB |
| 2,000 × `1e10` or `1.5` | 0.38 G fuel | unchanged |
