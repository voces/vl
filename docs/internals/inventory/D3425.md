### D3425 — a decimal integer literal past the i64 range keeps its low 64 bits with no refusal

**closed 2026-09-30 — now a loud check reject (`integer literal 100000000000000000000 overflows i64`) wherever no float destination holds the literal exactly; `-9223372036854775808` stays the least i64, and `const f: f64 = 100000000000000000000` still runs · a constant computed past the range says `constant … overflows i64` at its use · fixtures `tests/cases/literals/err-decimal-past-i64.vl`, `tests/cases/numerics/error-const-exact-overflow.vl` · was check-clean silently wrong · clause 1 · found by the exact-constant lane (owner ruling A) · filed 2026-09-30 against master `5e277ab52`**

Repro:

    print(100000000000000000000)
    // PRINTS 7766279631452241920

#### Mechanism

A decimal literal that does not fit `i32` types `i64` by its magnitude alone, and nothing asked
whether it fits `i64`: `parseI64Pair` folds the digits modulo 2^64 and the emitter writes that
`i64.const`. A radix literal wider than 64 bits was already refused at itself; a decimal one had no
such check. The ruling that a constant's use must hold its exact value makes the gap visible, since
a folded constant past the range is written as such a literal. The check now runs once every float
destination has taken the literals it holds exactly (`cexCheckWideLits`), and a negated `2^63` is
exempt.
