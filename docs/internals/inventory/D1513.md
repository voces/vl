#### Mechanism

**IT IS AN UNBOUNDED ALLOCATION, NOT ONE OVERSIZED ONE, AND THE TRAP'S OWN NOTE SAYS THE
OPPOSITE.** `vl build` runs the seed under wasmtime's NULL collector (`gc_engine`, `main.rs`),
which never frees, so `Trap::AllocationTooLarge` here means the 4 GiB GC heap FILLED — the host's
gloss ("larger than the GC heap can ever hold") describes a different failure. Measured rather
than read: `print(1)` costs 25 MB and the witness 4.22 GB, allocating steadily until the
heap is full. Every top frame the minimiser produced — `renderFaithful`, `__utf8_enc__`,
`arrElemNameRaw` — is a small allocation that happened to be the one that could not fit, which
is why chasing the frame found three different "sites" and none of them was the bug.

**THE LADDER RE-ENTERS ITSELF.** `globalCellKind` is a ~40-rung ladder over one module global,
and its rungs (`letIsRefArray`, `exprStruct`, `exprIsF64`, `exprRefArray`, …) reach any global
the initializer REFERENCES — coming back through `globalIsNulRefArraySid` /
`globalStructIndexSid` into `globalCellKind`. Nothing memoized, so each rung re-derived the
whole referenced chain: cost multiplies per link rather than adding. `globalKind`'s own header
already recorded the assumption this violates — "may recurse into another global, terminating
for an acyclic program". It does terminate. It is the CONSTANT that is the defect.

**MEASURED ON BOTH AXES** (peak RSS, `/usr/bin/time -f %M`, on the fixpoint seeds):

| axis | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| alias links 0 → 5 | 72 MB · 186 MB · 691 MB · 2.87 GB · **TRAP** · TRAP | 29 MB, FLAT at every length |
| `+` terms 1 → 5, over a 2-link chain | 1.04 GB · **TRAP** · TRAP · TRAP · TRAP | 31 · 40 · 49 · 60 · 73 MB |

(`terms` 6 → 8 continue 89 · 107 · 128 MB, and `print(1)` is 25 MB on both seeds.) Both axes
are `/usr/bin/time -f %M` on the two fixpoint seeds, which is load-independent — the elapsed
times on this box are not, and the trapping cells took 8 s to 46 s depending on contention.

The second axis is why the first fix attempt was not enough: a within-walk-only memo made the
alias chain linear and still trapped at seven `+` terms, because every REFERENCE in an
expression opened a fresh walk. The shipped memo survives across walks while its generation
does, and both axes are then linear.

**IT IS NOT THE D1500 SHAPE.** [D1440](D1440.md), [D1462](D1462.md) and [D1500](D1500.md) are
one shape at three sites — an unguarded `<table>[<expr>]` whose index came from a reader with
an in-band "no answer" value — and the fix each time is to bound the read. There is no
sentinel and no table read here: every index is in range and every rung returns a real answer.
What is unbounded is how MANY times they run. A fourth site of that shape would have been
found by auditing the readers; this one could only be found by measuring the cost.

#### Ablation

The row that named this cell claimed four ingredients, "remove any one and it runs". **Three of
the four are wrong, and the fourth is not an ingredient but a multiplier.** Graded one change
at a time against the BEFORE fixpoint seed, with peak RSS beside the verdict, because the
verdict is only "did this variant's cost cross 4 GiB":

| variant (one change from the witness) | before | before cost | after cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the witness | **TRAP** | 4.22 GB | 47 MB |
| inner list NOT inferred-empty (`[{y:1,x:2}]`) | **TRAP** | 4.22 GB | 45 MB |
| inner list ANNOTATED `P[]` | **TRAP** | 4.22 GB | 46 MB |
| a `for`-in over `v` ADDED | **TRAP** | 4.22 GB | 49 MB |
| `const s` instead of `let s` | **TRAP** | 4.22 GB | 48 MB |
| two `+` terms, not three | runs | 3.24 GB | 40 MB |
| one link fewer (`xs[0][0]`, no `v`) | runs | 670 MB | 42 MB |
| no bound read (`p` inlined) | runs | 388 MB | 47 MB |
| no NESTING (`v = __xs`) | runs | 37 MB | 26 MB |
| all of it inside a `function` | runs | 26 MB | 26 MB |

So the inferred-empty inner is **not** an ingredient (the annotated twin traps), the `for`-in is
**not** an ingredient (the minimal witness has none), and `let` is not either. What the four
"runs" rows share is not a missing ingredient — it is a cost 15× to 130× a normal program's,
sitting just under the ceiling. The last row is the real boundary: the identical statements
inside a `function` cost 26 MB, because a local rides `buildLocals`, not this ladder.
