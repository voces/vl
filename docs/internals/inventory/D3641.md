#### Mechanism

Not a miscompile: `s[i]` is the UTF-8 byte at byte offset `i` by design
(`docs/guide/strings-design.md`), and `for cp in s` is the code-point loop. sunpa's lettering
looked glyphs up per byte (`glyphOf(face, text[i])`, a parameter named `cp`), so `í`
(bytes 195, 173) became two wrong glyphs, and its capitals needed a local `upper` because
`toUpperAscii` stops at ASCII. Nothing in the checker could say so: a byte and a code point
are both `i32`. The close adds the two `std:str` exports the slip wanted, and a lint that
fires where a string's byte flows directly into a code-point position. The lint reads the
check's types when its caller paired the two (`vl check`, the corpus), and otherwise (the
editor, which lints before it checks) a receiver that is visibly a `string`: a literal, a
template, a name declared `string` or initialised from one, or a `.slice` of one.

#### Grading list

- The two compiler sites the lint found on first run, `nameStripSpaces` (`compiler/tyname.vl`)
  and `fmtImport` (`compiler/format.vl`), now copy byte slices rather than rebuilding a
  character from a byte; both inputs are ASCII today, so neither changed an output.
- The editor face: `tests/lsp_wasm_checker_test.ts` pins the finding and its range through
  `wasmChecker.lint`, unpaired with a check.
- The lexer's "Unexpected character 'Ã'" for `ï` outside a string was the same slip one
  hop away (the byte reached `charDesc` through a local, which the lint by design does not
  follow); it now names the whole character, `tests/cases/lexer/unexpected-char-non-ascii.vl`.
