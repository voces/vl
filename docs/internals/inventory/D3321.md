### D3321 — a literal `const` too wide for any destination is refused at its declaration and again at every read

**loud check reject · the refusal is right and owed; the defect is that it is reported twice, once per place the literal now stands · a price of D3246 (master `e70102886` reports it once, at the declaration) · fixture `tests/cases/literals/err-hex-past-64-bits.vl` · found in the #3296 round-4 review · filed 2026-09-29 against #3296 head `fcb70b50e`**

Repro:

    // Seventeen significant hex digits are 65 bits and no VL destination is that
    // wide, so the literal is refused at itself rather than silently keeping its
    // low 64 bits.
    const c = 0x1_0000_0000_0000_0000
    print(c)
    // vl check: rc 1, "0x1_0000_0000_0000_0000 does not fit a 64-bit destination"
    // at 4:11 (the declaration) AND at 5:7 (the read).  Want: the first only.

#### Mechanism

A literal `const` is its literal at every read (D3246), so each read of `c` is checked as the
literal written there and raises the literal's own error again. A literal that fits no
destination at all is already refused at the declaration, so its reads owe no second report:
the fix is to skip rewriting reads of a `const` whose declaration was refused for its width, or
to mark such a literal once and leave its copies silent.

#### Ablation

| ingredient changed | outcome | needed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| no read of `c` | one report, at the declaration | **yes** |
| `const c = 3000000000` read as `i32` | one report, at the read (the owed per-read error) | **yes** (a literal no width holds) |
