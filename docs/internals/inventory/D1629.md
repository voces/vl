#### Mechanism

`arrNewIntrKind` picks the right kind here — `exprArray` claims the `[0, 0]` literal and the
call lowers as kind 3, the ref list — and the fill LITERAL is then emitted at the outer list's
heap types. The disassembly says it in one line: the module interns `$0 = (array (mut i32))`
with wrapper `$3` for `i32[]` and `$4 = (array (mut (ref null $3)))` with wrapper `$5` for
`i32[][]`, and the emitted fill is `(struct.new $5 (array.new_fixed $4 2 (i32.const 0)
(i32.const 0)))` — the OUTER backing, whose slots are inner-list wrappers, filled with two
i32s. The element's own pair `$0`/`$3` is interned and never used. The annotation is what
supplies that rep: with `rows` inferred the literal is typed from itself and runs, and naming
the literal first (`const s: i32[] = [0, 0]`) gives it a binding of its own and runs. The
`@hint redundant type annotation` on this exact line is the checker agreeing about the TYPE
while the two spellings disagree about the REP.
