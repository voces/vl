### D3482 — `const xs: f32[] = [16777217]` is refused, by design, but the refusal names the list where the ruling names the constant

**loud check reject (`cannot assign i32[] to 'xs' of type f32[]`) · BY DESIGN: 16777217 is not exact at `f32`, and an integer literal is exactness-gated (DECISIONS.md, the f32 literal rule and "an integer constant at `f32` or `f64` … refused otherwise", D1890) · open only for the wording · found by the #3329 review · filed 2026-10-03 against master `a8c4dce6d`**

Repro:

    const xs: f32[] = [16777217]
    print(xs[0])
    // vl check: "cannot assign i32[] to 'xs' of type f32[]"

#### Mechanism

The refusal itself is the design: 2^24 + 1 has no `f32`, and a VL integer literal does not round,
so the escape hatches are `16777217.0` (a float literal, rounding once to 16777216) or `as f32`.
The scalar spelling `const x: f32 = 16777217` is refused with a sentence that says why (`i32
doesn't fit in f32 — the conversion is lossy`), and the exact-constants ruling says a refusal at a
list element names the constant and the destination (`constant 16777217 is not exact at f32`).
The list spelling instead types the literal `i32[]` and refuses the whole assignment, which names
neither. A close words the element's refusal; the program stays refused.

#### Ablation
