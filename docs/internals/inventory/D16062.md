#### Mechanism

`isValueUnionName` grades a union by how its site's spelling writes each member,
and the spellings disagree with each other:

- **Key text** (`canonUnionKeyTextOfTy`): a literal member is written at its
  base, with no dedup, so `"a" | 1` reads `string|i32` and `"a" | "b"` reads
  `string|string`. A run that regroups to a declared alias (`K|i32`) or a quoted
  inline group is no atom.
- **`unionSpellingOfTy` and the inferred-return names**: a literal set is
  written as its alias or a quoted group, which `valueAtomKind` calls no atom.
  The literal-arm path, `nameIsLitUnionArmValueUnion`, boxes such a union
  instead.

`isValueUnionTyOr` follows neither convention: it reads literals at their base
only when they share one. It answers no for the witness's `"a" | 1`, which
`isValueUnionBox` and D3491 say boxes. Row minting, `is` dispatch and box
destinations are built on the spellings' answers. So asking the type with its
one rule, as `repOf(ty)` should, loses programs until those consumers agree on
one answer.
