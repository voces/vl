#### Mechanism

`parseUnary` handled a prefix operator by recursing into `parseUnary` for its operand, and that
recursion consumed the operand's whole `as` chain, so the operator was applied to the cast's
result. The guide's precedence paragraph already said unary operators bind tighter than `as`;
the parser disagreed. The fix splits the layer in three: `parsePrefixed` (prefix operators over
a postfix operand and its type guard, no casts), `parseCastChain`, and `parseGuardSuffix`, so
`!x is T` stays `!(x is T)` while `-x as T` is `(-x) as T`. The printer's cast arm prints a
prefix operand bare. The grid also exposed that `checkUnaryNode` let `~` through for any numeric
type, with the deferred pin constraint recorded as `OP_NEG`; `~` over an `f64`/`f32` now refuses
as the binary bitwise operators do, and the pin records `OP_BNOT`.
