Repro:

    function go() {
      const x: !string = 5

    }
    go()
    // vl check: rc 0.  vl run: prints 5.

#### Mechanism

A negation is a checker-only refinement with no positive type of its own, so it names no rep.
A binding annotated with one must therefore take the cell its INITIALIZER gives it — which is
what an un-annotated binding beside it already does, and what [D1775](D1775.md) established
for the module-global face. Three ladders read the annotation instead: `letCellKind`,
`globalHasTypeAnn`'s two callers (the global cell kind and its struct slot), and
`checkLocalI32`, whose `tyScalarBasePrim` gets neither `i32` nor `boolean` from a `TyNeg` and
refuses. All three now read through the negation, through one arena predicate `tyIsNegation`
and its two annotation-node wrappers; `globalHasTypeAnn` is renamed `globalAnnNamesCell`,
which is the question both of its callers were actually asking.

**The cell alone ships a wrong value, measured before shipping.** With the cell rung alone,
`const t: !string = true` moved from a loud refusal to printing `1` where its un-annotated
twin prints `true`: `print`'s arm is chosen by `boundBoolByName`, which short-circuits on the
annotation exactly as the cell ladder did. Cell and reader land together, and the reader half
also fixes a module global that printed `1` on master with no change of mine.
