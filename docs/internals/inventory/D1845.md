#### Mechanism

Two filters, one rule, spelled once each: `checkWhileStmtNode` kept only condition facts whose
key `!isPathKey`, and the two loop-body pushes held `narrowBareIdentOnly`, which
`pushNarrowRep` enforces at the single bottleneck every narrowing channel bottoms out at.
Neither half could narrow what the other did not, which is why the refusal was loud rather
than a rep disagreement — and why lifting it is both halves or neither.

It was the PATH that declined, not the `is` — `while o.v != null` refused too, and both `if`
spellings of both guards ran. The close is the row's own prediction, unchanged: pass every
collected fact to `applyNarrows` (the path arm already rides the `npKeys` overlay the body's
scope pushes) and stop holding `narrowBareIdentOnly` across `emitWhile` and
`collectLocalsWhile`. Nothing new on the retirement side — `pushReBarrier` runs before the
body's scope, so a fact the head contributes counts as inside the barrier.

**What makes it sound is `noteCallInvalidations`, and that is a fixture, not an argument.** The
restriction's stated reason was an alias hole in the `if` spelling
(`if o.v != null { clr(o); o.v.length }` "check-clean and TRAPS"); run today that program is a
loud check reject naming the call, because a call that can write the path retires the
narrowing. The loop takes the same channel: `error-while-path-call-retires.vl` is the refusal,
and the read after the call is at the declared type.
